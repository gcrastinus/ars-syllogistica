# Advanced Studies: plan

Prepared October 21, 2026, before any app code for Phase 3 was written. Timothy's design: each act opens with its doctrine deck, then its exercises (each with a lead-in); the Advanced Studies decks then perfect the skill. This plan lists, act by act, the topics, the source loci (Maritain first), what the course already teaches on each topic (by card title), what the Advanced deck adds, the cards, the question forms, the Review, and the paired exercise where one fits.

Page numbers are printed pages unless marked "pdf p." They belong in these notes and in the report, never in student text. Translators are named only in sources lines.

## 2. Binding decisions (from DECISION-SHEET.md and -2)

- Affirmatives carry existential import; negatives do not (§1). The ⊗ marks stay as decided; Camestres and Camenos keep their ⊗ with the one-sentence note on how the diagram writes O.
- Modal syllogisms follow Aristotle, *Prior Analytics* I.9: a necessary major with a plain minor in the first figure gives a necessary conclusion. Theophrastus's weaker-premise rule appears **only in one short note** (in the modal-syllogism deck), with Theophrastus introduced by appositive once.
- Contingent premises: only the cases in A1 are tested; the second figure with two contingent premises gives nothing.
- The tenth category is "having," with "(also called habit)" once at its first mention in each deck that names it; "posture" as decided.
- The fourth figure is "the later textbook account," which St. Thomas and John of St. Thomas treat as the indirect moods of the first figure (A5).
- Fallacies follow *De fallaciis*, always "attributed to St. Thomas." Definition and division follow St. Thomas and John of St. Thomas: "logical division" for genus into species; causal definition by the outside causes.
- Plain established terms; an established Latin name may appear once, in parentheses, after the English.

## 3. Decks and paired exercises

Fourteen decks and five paired exercise sets (XXII–XXVI). Every deck: 7–9 cards, three check questions per card (one form drawn at random; a wrong answer explained, then another form), a deck Review built by the existing `buildReview`, and a sources line on the first card (ECT article first, then `SRC_MAR`, then the others used). No check question uses its own card's example. The correct option is the longest option in at most a third of each deck's questions (checked by `tools/check.js`). No True/False questions.

### FIRST ACT

**What the course already teaches.** First Act deck: "What the first act is," "Signs," "The concept, a formal sign," "Words, concepts, and things," "The concept and the term," "Abstraction," "Comprehension and extension," "What follows in the First Act." Lead-in I: "The five predicables," "Two questions, and a test" (property converts; accident, separable and inseparable, with the raven), "Genus and species are relative" (predicables and categories; simple supposition named once). Lead-in II: "Substance and the nine accidents" (primary and secondary substance), "How the nine follow from substance," "Distinctions that are easily missed." Lead-in III: "What division is, and its kinds," "The rules of division." Lead-in IV: "The four kinds of definition," "The rules of definition."

**A1. The Predicables in Depth** (8 cards). Adds: direct and reflex knowledge as the ground of second intentions (Peifer; Minerd 3–6; Maritain pp. 1–2); Porphyry's tree with supreme genus, subaltern genera, lowest species, individuals (Houser pp. 109–111); Porphyry's four senses of "property" (Cajetan's text of Porphyry; *Material Logic* p. 604 n. 16); predicable accident and predicamental accident (Houser p. 115; *ST* I q. 77 a. 1 ad 5); the difference divides the genus and constitutes the species (Cajetan); Aristotle's four heads in the *Topics* (definition, property, genus, accident) beside Porphyry's five (*Topics* I.4, I.8; Houser p. 55); the three positions on the universal (Minerd 19–24; ECT III on nominalism). Cards: The universal known by reflection · The tree of Porphyry · Highest, middle, and lowest · Property in four senses · Two kinds of accident · The difference divides and constitutes · Aristotle's four and Porphyry's five · What the universal is.

**A2. The Categories in Depth** (8 cards). Adds the antepredicaments and postpredicaments, which no card yet teaches: univocal, equivocal, derivative names (*Categories* 1; Oesterle pp. 27–29); analogous names, by attribution and by proportion, and metaphor (Oesterle p. 29; Minerd 158–170); said of a subject and present in a subject, the fourfold division (*Categories* 2; Oesterle pp. 30–31); the five conditions for placing a nature in a category (Oesterle pp. 37–38); why the categories belong to logic and why being is not a genus (Oesterle p. 36; *ST* I q. 3 a. 5); the four kinds of opposition (Oesterle pp. 41–44); prior and simultaneous (*Categories* 12–13); the six kinds of motion (*Categories* 14). Lead-in II's derivation of the nine is not repeated.

**A3. Definition and Division in Depth** (7 cards). Adds: Aristotle's four questions ("Is it? What is it? Whether? Why?") and where definition stands among them (*Post. An.* II.1; Houser p. 54); division as the way to a definition, with Plato's angler (Houser pp. 113–114; Minerd 33–35); dividing by the proper differences of the genus and combining differences (Conway, "Two Methods for the Real Definition"); what cannot strictly be defined: highest genera and individuals (John of St. Thomas, *Outlines* pp. 47–48); definition through the four causes (Minerd 41, citing *ST* I-II q. 55 a. 4); definition and demonstration (the eclipse: the definition that is the conclusion, the one that is the middle, *Post. An.* II.10); the kinds of whole and the division of a term into its senses (*Outlines* pp. 49–50). Lead-in III and IV's rules and kinds are not repeated.

**A4. Supposition** (8 cards). Maritain pp. 59–76 throughout, with Klima–Noone III.F–H and Minerd 47–51. Lead-in I names simple supposition once. Cards: Signification and supposition · Material and formal, proper and improper · Simple and personal · Singular, determinate, distributed (descent by "or" and by "and") · Merely confused and collective · The predicate's supposition and the rules of distribution · The copula and existence (an affirmative whose subject stands for nothing is false; natural and accidental supposition; ampliation and restriction) · A change of supposition breaks the argument.

**Paired exercise XXII. Supposition.** An item bank of 36 sentences, each with one term marked, most from Maritain's own drill (p. 72) and Klima–Noone's examples, rewritten in the course's style; new items where needed. Question forms: "What supposition does the marked term have?" (options drawn from material, simple, personal singular, personal determinate, personal distributed, merely confused, collective, improper); "Which argument changes the supposition of its middle term?"; "Is this affirmative true if its subject stands for nothing?" Levels: I–II material, simple, personal; III adds singular, determinate, distributed; IV adds merely confused, collective, improper; V adds the arguments that change supposition. Built with `mc4Make` and the tier machinery of the predicables generator (`defTiers`, `recentPick`).

### SECOND ACT

**What the course already teaches.** Second Act deck: "Composing and dividing," "The proposition," "Quality and quantity," "The four forms: A, E, I, O," "Truth is in the proposition," "Existential import," "Opposition," "What follows in the Second Act." Lead-in VI: "The square of opposition," "Converse, obverse, contrapositive, contradictory." Lead-in VII: "Mode and dictum," "The modal square," "The composite and the divided sense." Lead-in XII and XIII teach the conjunctive, disjunctive, and conditional *syllogisms*, not the propositions.

**B1. Opposition and Conversion in Depth** (9 cards). Adds: contradiction is the strongest opposition and subalternation is not opposition properly (Maritain pp. 132–134); singular propositions and a universal taken as one admit only contradiction (p. 131); necessary matter, where contraries cannot both be false (p. 135 n.); future contingents (pp. 135–136; *De interpretatione* 9 and St. Thomas's commentary, lect. 13–14); conversion rests on supposition (Klima–Noone III.H; Maritain p. 142); Buridan's limits on contraposition and obversion when a term stands for nothing (Klima–Noone III.H), consistent with Decision §1; conversion is not inference in the proper sense (Maritain pp. 162–168); the reciprocal and the inverse (pp. 145–147); the modern reading of "All S are P" as a universally quantified conditional, which represents but does not say what the proposition means (ECT IV–V; Klima–Noone III.D–E: factual and law-like universals).

**B2. The Modal Proposition in Depth** (7 cards). Adds: modes of the copula and modes that affect only the subject or predicate (Maritain pp. 112–113); quality and quantity of a modal (p. 137); the eight-corner square with quantified dicta and "impossible that every man run" (pp. 139–140); the vowels A, E, I, U once, in parentheses (pp. 140–141); *de dicto* and *de re* (St. Thomas, *Super Sent.* I d. 38 q. 1 a. 5 ad 5; *De veritate* q. 2 a. 12 ad 4; Decision Sheet §2); one-sided and two-sided possibility (*Prior Analytics* I.13 and I.15; Decision Sheet-2 A1); the mode belongs to the proposition, not to the mind's act of judging (Maritain p. 114, against Kant), and conversion keeps the mode (p. 144).

**B3. Compound and Exponible Propositions** (8 cards). Maritain pp. 99–110 and 135 with John of St. Thomas pp. 95–98 and Minerd 146. Cards: Simple and compound; openly and hiddenly compound · The copulative · The disjunctive, proper and improper, and the conjunctive · The conditional: true when the sequence holds · Causal, rational, relative, adversative · The contradictory of a compound · Exclusive and exceptive propositions · Reduplicative propositions.

**Paired exercise XXIII. Compound and Exponible Propositions.** An item bank of 40: classify a proposition (copulative, disjunctive, conjunctive, conditional, causal, exclusive, exceptive, reduplicative); say when it is true or false; give its contradictory; give the propositions an exponible resolves into. Levels: I–II classify openly compound; III adds the hiddenly compound and truth conditions; IV adds contradictories and exponents; V adds reduplicatives and mixed cases. Reuses `mc4Make` and the tier filter.

### THIRD ACT

**What the course already teaches.** Third Act deck: "What reasoning is," "The syllogism, broad and narrow," "Three terms and two premises" (the figures named), "The middle term and the cause," "Valid, sound, and demonstrative," "Induction and demonstration," "What follows in the Third Act." What Logic Is: "The parts of logic: demonstration" (quia and propter quid in two sentences). Lead-ins VIII–XI: "Three circles for three terms," "The ⊗," "Which moods take a ⊗," "Terms and figure," "Distribution," "The general rules," "The rules, in brief," "Finding the conclusion," "When nothing follows." XVI: "The question of mode," "Contingent premises," "A note on another view" (Theophrastus). XVII: "A chain of syllogisms." XVIII: "Form and matter," "Soundness and demonstration." XXI: "What a fallacy is," "The thirteen fallacies," "Disputations and the sophist's aims."

**C1. Figures and Moods** (8 cards). Maritain pp. 185–209. Adds: figure and mood defined; why 64 combinations give 19 moods; the special rules of each figure with the principle each depends on (*dictum de omni et nullo*, *de diverso*, *de exemplo*); what each figure is good for (the second for refutation, the third for the counterexample, p. 205); the indirect moods and the later account's fourth figure (pp. 186–190; Decision A5); singular premises (pp. 116–117, 190 n.); the essence of the syllogism is the identity of two terms with a third, not a descent from universal to particular (pp. 206–209); Barbara and Celarent as the moods of science (Conway). Moods are named English first ("AAA in the first figure"), with the Latin name once in parentheses.

**C2. Reduction** (7 cards). Maritain pp. 200–205, 189 n. Cards: Perfect and imperfect moods · What the letters mean · Direct reduction by simple conversion and transposition · Reduction by conversion by limitation · Reduction to the impossible · The indirect moods reduced · Valid in their own right.

**Paired exercise XXIV. Figures, Moods, and Reduction.** A generator over `VALID` (24 valid mood-figure pairs) using `makeTerms`, `propText`, and `premiseTerms`: name the figure; name the mood ("AAA in the first figure (Barbara)"); name the first-figure mood it reduces to; say what a given operation does (convert simply, convert by limitation, transpose the premises, reduce to the impossible); name the premise that is replaced in a reduction to the impossible. Over 100 distinct items. Levels: I figure (figures 1–3); II mood (figures 1–2); III reduction target, direct moods; IV the operations; V reduction to the impossible and the indirect moods.

**C3. The Modal Syllogism in Depth** (7 cards). Adds: why the first-figure rule holds (*Prior Analytics* I.9); why a plain major with a necessary minor gives only a plain conclusion (I.9); the necessity of the consequence and of the consequent (St. Thomas, *Expositio Posteriorum* I, lect. 14, n. 5); *de dicto* and *de re* in a syllogism (*Super Sent.* I d. 38 ad 5); converting modal premises (I.3; the contingent universal negative does not convert, I.17); one-sided possibility in some conclusions (I.15); the short note on Theophrastus's rule and on the medieval qualification that the plain minor be true "simply" (Decision Sheet §2, SEP on Kilwardby); Maritain's "the logician's cross" (p. 249).

**C4. Compound Arguments** (8 cards). Maritain pp. 247–258. Cards: The epicheirema · Polysyllogism and sorites · The Aristotelian and the Goclenian sorites · The dilemma · The three rules of a dilemma · Retorting a dilemma · Dilemma and disjunctive syllogism · Oblique syllogisms (pp. 250–252).

**Paired exercise XXV. Compound Arguments.** An item bank of 32: name the kind (epicheirema, polysyllogism, Aristotelian sorites, Goclenian sorites, dilemma, disjunctive syllogism, enthymeme); say which rule a faulty dilemma breaks; retort a dilemma; find the hidden syllogism in an epicheirema's causal premise. Levels by tier, as above.

**C5. Induction and Example** (8 cards). Maritain pp. 161–162, 258–287. Cards: Two ways to make truth known · No middle term · "Every," not "all" · Complete and incomplete, sufficient and insufficient · The two rules · Induction is not a syllogism in Darapti · Reasoning by resemblance · Analogy in reasoning and in knowledge.

**C6. Demonstration** (8 cards). St. Thomas, *Expositio Posteriorum* I with Conway and Minerd 172–183. Cards: The conditions of the premises (lect. 4) · First and second per se modes (lect. 10) · Third and fourth per se modes, and the per accidens · Through the effect: the planets (lect. 23) · Through the remote cause: the wall (lect. 24) · Proper principles, not common ones: Bryson (Conway) · The first figure is the figure of science · Science of the fact and science of the reasoned fact in different sciences (subalternation).

**Paired exercise XXVI. Demonstration.** An item bank of 30: *quia* through an effect, *quia* through a remote cause, or *propter quid*; which per se mode a predication shows (or per accidens); which condition of demonstrative premises a failed proof breaks. Levels by tier.

**C7. The Fallacies in Depth** (9 cards). *De fallaciis* throughout, with John of St. Thomas pp. 134–136. Cards: Three kinds of multiplicity · The modes of equivocation and amphiboly · Composition, division, and accent · The modes of figure of speech · The seven outside the words, and why seven · Accident and consequent · Five ways to beg the question · Non-cause and many questions · Every fallacy is an ignorance of the refutation. Set XXI keeps its own bank; no new set.

## 4. Home, Guided Path, other pages
