# Aristotelian Logic by Direct Instruction — Master Plan

A 30-lesson Direct Instruction (Engelmann-style) program teaching the first and second acts of the mind, preparing two students (boy 9, girl 12, no prior knowledge) for third-act work in the Ars Syllogistica app.

**Primary source:** Andres, *Intro to Aristotelian Logic* (Prologue + Lessons 1–11, with Lesson 12's conversion section).
**Enrichment/support:** Poinsot, *Outlines of Formal Logic*; John of St. Thomas, *Material Logic*; Kearns, "Logic (General and Historical)," ect.org.
**Practice environment:** Ars Syllogistica (index.html). See 06-Ars-Syllogistica-Alignment-Notes.md.

---

## 1. Delivery model

- Both children taught together, one script per session, ~20–30 minutes daily.
- Differentiation by extension, not by separate tracks: each script ends with 2–3 "Deeper" items for the 12-year-old while the 9-year-old does firming review.
- Every 5th lesson is a mastery test with specified remedies. Nothing new is introduced until the tested material is firm (100% on core items).
- App work begins mid-Unit 1 and runs alongside lessons thereafter; the app's own pass/mastery/spaced-review machinery (9 of last 10 at Level III+ across two days to pass; Level V for mastery) replaces paper review once a set is reached.

## 2. Direct Instruction design commitments

These bind every script. They are restated in 01-Script-Format-and-Standing-Prompt.md as instructions to the drafting models.

1. **Faultless communication.** Every new concept is taught through a sequence of examples and non-examples that admits only one interpretation. Positive examples show the greatest possible variety in irrelevant features; negative examples are minimally different from positives so the discrimination is unmistakable.
2. **Model – Lead – Test.** New skill: teacher models with exact wording, leads chorally, tests individually. Scripts print teacher words verbatim in bold, expected student responses after an arrow, and the correction procedure for each likely error.
3. **Small steps, massed firming, distributed review.** No more than ~15% of any lesson is new. Every concept re-appears in at least the next five lessons (a "track"), then intermittently forever. Review items are cumulative and interleaved, never blocked by type.
4. **Signals and pacing.** Choral response on a signal (tap or hand-drop), think-time before the signal, brisk pacing, individual turns to catch passengers. Corrections are neutral and immediate: model → test → delayed retest later in the same lesson.
5. **Wording constancy.** Definitions and rules are taught with fixed wording and never paraphrased by the teacher until students are firm. The fixed wordings are set in this plan (Section 4) and drawn from the sources; the models write new connective and instructional text around them but do not alter the canonical wordings.
6. **Preparation for the third act.** Every unit plants seeds the syllogism will need: subject/predicate analysis, the universal, "said of every / said of none," standard form, letters as schematic terms, and truth-transmission (opposition and conversion as the first taste of "if this is true, that must be").

## 3. Program map

### Unit 0 — Orientation and the Meno (Lessons 1–3)
The Meno is reviewed, not memorized: its key lessons are drawn out and *those lessons* are drilled, not the dialogue.

- **L1. What Logic Is.** Logic as the art of reasoning well; words–thoughts–things; the mind-and-hand tool analogy; the three acts of the mind (apprehension, judgment/composing-and-dividing, reasoning). Sources: Andres Prologue; ECT article.
- **L2. The Meno I — What Is Virtue?** Socrates's question; Meno's swarm of virtues; the one-over-many (all bees are alike as bees). Lesson drawn: an answer to "What is X?" must be one, and must fit every X.
- **L3. The Meno II — Testing Answers.** Counterexample method; a definition must include every instance and exclude everything else; from known to unknown (slave-boy scene, previewed lightly — it returns in L28). Lessons drawn become the two Definition Rules used all program long.

### Unit 1 — The First Act: Simple Apprehension (Lessons 4–15)

- **L4. Subject and Predicate; the Universal.** Andres Lesson 2. The universal: a word that can be predicated of more than one subject. Singular vs universal terms (feeds the app's letters convention: capitals for general terms, lower-case/proper names for singulars).
- **L5. Genus and Species.** Andres Lesson 3. Tree diagrams (living thing → animal → dog).
- **L6. Difference, Property, Accident.** Andres Lesson 3 completed. The five predicables assembled.
- **L7. Predicables Firming + Mastery Test 1.** App onboarding: tracking on; begin **Predicables** set (non-gating; ongoing).
- **L8. Division.** Kinds and rules of division (enrichment from John of St. Thomas / Poinsot on the conditions of good division). App: begin **Division** set (gates Act II in the app).
- **L9. Definition.** Genus + specific difference; the two Meno rules formalized; rules of good definition (Poinsot enrichment: not wider, not narrower, clearer than the defined, no defining by negation without need). App: begin **Definition** set (gates Act II).
- **L10. Using Names; the Ante-Predicaments.** Andres Lesson 4: equivocal, univocal, denominative naming; why a list of highest genera (categories) is needed.
- **L11. Substance.** Andres Lesson 5. First and second substance; substance vs accident.
- **L12. The Nine Accidents.** Andres Lesson 6. Quantity, quality, relation, action, passion, when, where, position, habit — taught by sorting, not by memorized list first.
- **L13. Categories Firming.** App: begin **Categories** set (non-gating; ongoing).
- **L14. The Post-Predicaments (light).** Andres Lesson 7, oral treatment only: the four opposites (correlatives, contraries, possession/privation, affirmation/negation — the last previews Act II), prior, simultaneous.
- **L15. Mastery Test 2 + App Checkpoint.** Target: Division and Definition sets passed in-app (opens the app's Second Act).

### Unit 2 — The Second Act: Composing and Dividing (Lessons 16–27)

- **L16. Noun and Verb.** Andres Lesson 8. Simple vs complex expressions; noun signifies without time, verb with time; indefinite nouns.
- **L17. The Statement.** Andres Lesson 8. Only the statement is true or false; statement vs question, command, wish. Truth as saying-of-what-is-that-it-is.
- **L18. Quality: Affirmation and Denial.** Andres Lesson 9. Composing vs dividing; every affirmation has its proper denial.
- **L19. Quantity: the Four Statement Types.** Andres Lesson 9. Universal, particular, singular, indefinite; A, E, I, O introduced with the traditional vowels (from *AffIrmo* and *nEgO* — enrichment from Poinsot).
- **L20. Standard Form; "Proposition."** Putting ordinary sentences into quantifier + subject + copula + predicate form ("All dogs are things that bark"). Teach the mapping: the statement, or proposition — Andres's own equivalence — so the app's vocabulary lands as a synonym, not a new thing. App: begin **Diagramming Propositions** prep (diagrams come in L25).
- **L21. Opposition I.** Andres Lesson 10. Contradictories and contraries; truth rules (contradictories always divide true/false; contraries cannot both be true, can both be false).
- **L22. Opposition II — The Square.** Subcontraries, subalternation, the full square assembled. App: **Square of Opposition** lab (study section, unscored) alongside.
- **L23. Conversion.** Andres Lesson 12 (conversion section). Simple conversion of E and I; conversion of A only to I (per accidens); O does not convert. First truth-transmission drills: TRUE / FALSE / UNKNOWN.
- **L24. Obversion and Contraposition.** App conventions (needed to pass the app's **Immediate Inference** set, which gates Act III). Taught as later scholastic refinements built on the square plus indefinite (negative) terms from L16. Sourced from the app's own study text; flag to students as "extra tools the app adds."
- **L25. Diagramming Propositions.** The app's Venn conventions: shading = empty, × = occupant, ⊗ = traditional existential import. Taught as pictures of what A, E, I, O say. App: **Diagramming Propositions** set in earnest.
- **L26. The Modal Statements.** Necessary, impossible, possible, contingent. Beyond Andres; drawn from Poinsot's treatment of modals; required because the app's **Modal Propositions** set gates Act III (see alignment notes for the suggested app change). Extension-heavy lesson: core for both children, equipollences for the 12-year-old.
- **L27. Mastery Test 3 + App Checkpoint.** Target: all three Second Act sets passed (opens the app's Third Act).

### Unit 3 — Bridge to the Third Act (Lessons 28–30)

- **L28. From Known to Unknown.** Andres Prologue + Meno slave-boy scene revisited as reasoning in action; what an argument is; premises and conclusion as words.
- **L29. First Sight of the Syllogism.** Three terms, three statements, the middle term as the "go-between"; letters as schematic terms (matching the app's letters mode); the three figures named, with a one-panel note that the app also offers a "fourth figure" — a scholastic convention; Aristotle and our books count three (full treatment deferred to third-act study).
- **L30. Mastery Test 4 + Readiness Review.** Cumulative test over all four units; tour of the app's **Diagram Workshop**; celebrate the opened Third Act.

## 4. Canonical wordings (fixed across all scripts)

Scripts must use these exact formulations once introduced; models write around them, never over them.

- Logic is the art of reasoning well.
- There is a twofold operation of the intellect... one is the understanding of simple objects... the other is the operation of composing and dividing. There is also a third operation, that of reasoning, by which reason proceeds from what is known to the investigation of things that are unknown.
- Logic is about words insofar as they signify things through our thoughts.
- The subject is that of which something is affirmed or denied; the predicate is what is affirmed or denied of the subject.
- A universal is a word that can be predicated of more than one subject.
- A genus is a kind of thing that has other kinds underneath it.
- A definition must include every instance of the thing defined, and it must show how that kind of thing is different from other, similar things.
- The noun is a simple expression that has no reference to time; the verb signifies with time.
- Only the statement is either true or false.
- The affirmation joins together the subject and predicate; the denial separates them. (Quality)
- Universal statements take the universal subject in all of its universality; particular statements take it only in part. (Quantity)
- In every conversion, the new statement has the same quality as the old statement, but it sometimes differs in quantity.

Terminology decisions: say **statement** through L19; from L20 onward "statement, or proposition" until firm, then either freely. Say **denial** first, "negation" as synonym at L18. The categories keep Andres's English names. A/E/I/O vowel names are core; their Latin mnemonic origin is enrichment.

## 5. Tracks (strands woven across lessons)

Each runs as 1–3 minutes of review in every lesson after introduction: T1 vocabulary/definitions (L1→); T2 subject–predicate parsing (L4→); T3 predicables sorting (L5→); T4 genus-tree building (L5→); T5 categories sorting (L10→); T6 definition testing by counterexample (L3→); T7 statement classification A/E/I/O (L19→); T8 square inferences TRUE/FALSE/UNKNOWN (L21→); T9 standard-form translation (L20→); T10 app-skills (letters mode, diagram marks) (L7→).

## 6. Division of labor among the tools

- **Claude (this session):** program architecture, briefs, QA against the PDFs, final consistency passes if wanted.
- **Opus 5 — the drafter (recommended).** One chat per unit; standing prompt + format once at the top, then one brief per lesson → complete script in a single pass, including its own spare items and self-check flags (see file 01). Chosen over Grok because the scripts turn on exact canonical wordings and genuinely minimal-difference example sequences — precision work.
- **Grok 4.6 — optional reinforcement.** Extra worksheet/flashcard variants at volume, and fresh-eyes critique of the four mastery-test lessons or any script that taught badly (a model critiques another's work more ruthlessly than its own). See file 01, Section C.
- **Your read-through is the quality gate;** the classroom is the real test. Failed lessons go back to the same Opus chat with a description of what broke.
- **Ars Syllogistica:** all post-teaching practice, pass/mastery certification, and spaced review from L7 onward, per the checkpoints above.
