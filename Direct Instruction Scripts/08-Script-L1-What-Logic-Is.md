## 2. NEW MATERIAL (12 min) — three short cycles

### 2A. What logic is, and why we need it

**MODEL**

**An art is a way of doing something well.**

**Logic is the art of reasoning well.**

(Hold up your open hand, then the pencil.)

**The hand can do almost anything, but few things well. So the hand makes tools — a knife, a shovel, a pencil — to work well.**

**The mind is like the hand. The mind can do almost anything, but it works well when it makes tools for itself.**

**The mind's tools are precise words, statements, and arguments.**

**Logic studies how to make those tools well. That is why we need logic.**

**Listen again. What is logic? Logic is the art of reasoning well.**

**LEAD** (teacher and students together)

**What is logic?** (pause; Signal)
→ Logic is the art of reasoning well.

**Again. What is logic?** (pause; Signal)
→ Logic is the art of reasoning well.

**Why does the mind need logic?** (pause; Signal)
→ The mind makes tools so it can work well.

**TEST** (students only)

**What is logic?** (pause; Signal)
→ Logic is the art of reasoning well.

**(9), what is logic?**
→ Logic is the art of reasoning well.

**(12), what is logic?**
→ Logic is the art of reasoning well.

**Why does the mind need logic?** (pause; Signal)
→ The mind makes tools so it can work well.

**(12), why does the mind need logic?**
→ The mind makes tools so it can work well.

**(9), the hand makes a knife. What does the mind make?**
→ precise words, statements, and arguments
(Accept: words, statements, and arguments.)

#### CORRECTION — 2A

### 2B. What logic is about

(Write on the board, well spaced, and leave it up for the rest of the lesson:)

```
WORDS  →  THOUGHTS  →  THINGS
```

**MODEL**

**I say the word dog.** (point to WORDS) **That word puts a thought of a dog in your mind.** (point to THOUGHTS) **That thought is like the real dog.** (point to THINGS)

**Words signify thoughts. Thoughts are likenesses of things.**

**Logic is not about ink or noise. Logic is about words insofar as they signify things through our thoughts.**

**Say that with me in parts.**

**Logic is about words** (pause; Signal)
→ Logic is about words

**insofar as they signify things** (pause; Signal)
→ insofar as they signify things

**through our thoughts.** (pause; Signal)
→ through our thoughts.

**The whole thing. Logic is about words insofar as they signify things through our thoughts.**

**LEAD**

**What is logic about?** (pause; Signal)
→ Logic is about words insofar as they signify things through our thoughts.

**Again. What is logic about?** (pause; Signal)
→ Logic is about words insofar as they signify things through our thoughts.

**I say horse. The word horse — what does it put in your mind?** (pause; Signal)
→ a thought of a horse
(Accept: a thought / a thought of a horse.)

**That thought is like what?** (pause; Signal)
→ the real horse
(Accept: a horse / the thing.)

**TEST**

**What is logic about?** (pause; Signal)
→ Logic is about words insofar as they signify things through our thoughts.

**(9), what is logic about?**

**(12), what is logic about?**

### 2C. The three acts of the mind

**MODEL**

**The old books say this. There is a twofold operation of the intellect... one is the understanding of simple objects, that is, the operation by which the intellect apprehends just the essence of a thing alone; the other is the operation of composing and dividing. There is also a third operation, that of reasoning, by which reason proceeds from what is known to the investigation of things that are unknown.**

**We will say it this way.**

(Write and leave up:)

```
ACT ONE — grasping what a thing is
ACT TWO — putting thoughts together to say true or false
ACT THREE — reasoning from what we know to what we don't yet know
```

**Act one — grasping what a thing is.**

**Listen. Dog.** (pause) **That is act one. I am only grasping what a thing is.**

**Apple.** (pause) **Act one.**

**Hammer.** (pause) **Act one.**

**Act two — putting thoughts together to say true or false.**

**Dogs are animals.** (pause) **That can be true or false. That is act two.**

**Apples are fruits.** (pause) **Act two.**

**Listen to the difference. Dog.** (pause) **Act one. Dogs are animals.** (pause) **Act two.**

**Act three — reasoning from what we know to what we don't yet know.**

**All animals move themselves, so dogs move themselves.** (pause) **I used what I know to get to something further. That is act three.**

**All tools help us work, a hammer is a tool, so a hammer helps us work.** (pause) **Act three.**

**Some things are not a statement about what is.**

**Is the dog outside?** (pause) **That is a question. Not a statement about what is.**

**Sit!** (pause) **That is a command. Not a statement about what is.**

**When I ask which act, say ACT ONE, ACT TWO, ACT THREE, or NOT A STATEMENT ABOUT WHAT IS.**

**LEAD** (teacher joins on the first three, then fades)

**Dog.** (pause; Signal)
→ ACT ONE

**Dogs are animals.** (pause; Signal)
→ ACT TWO

**All animals move themselves, so dogs move themselves.** (pause; Signal)

## 9. DRAFTER'S FLAGS

1. **L1 has no prior track.** Review is response-training plus word-vs-thing, not a fake logic review. Word-vs-thing is the seed for 2B, so it is not extra doctrine.

2. **Three new concepts in one lesson.** The brief requires all three. Each cycle is short; the long work is the 2C sort, which is examples, not a fourth idea. If time runs long, cut the last two guided items, not the 2C test.

3. **"Statement" is used before L17.** The brief's own negative wording is "not a statement about what is." The script uses that exact phrase as the choral response so we do not teach the L17 rule (only statements are true or false). A 9-year-old might still treat any sentence as ACT TWO. Fix already applied: every question/command in the sequence sits next to a minimally different act-two item (*Dogs bark* / *Do dogs bark?*; *The hammer is a tool* / *Get the hammer!*).

4. **Act-three items could be heard as "just a long sentence."** Fix already applied: every act-three example includes *so*, and the correction names *so* as the audible mark of going further. *So* is not taught as the definition of reasoning.

5. **The long Aquinas sentence is said once, not memorized.** Children produce only the simplified three lines from the brief. The long form is present so the canonical wording is heard.

6. **"What is logic about?" is a heavy sentence for a 9-year-old.** Fix already applied: it is built in three chunks, then asked whole, then asked individually. Do not accept a shortened form in test.

7. **A command like *Sit!* might be called ACT ONE** because it is one word. Fix already applied: the correction distinguishes a command from grasping what a thing is; *Sit!* is paired later with the name *sister* / the statement *my sister is kind*.

8. **(12) deeper on *logos*.** The 9-year-old is not idle: he repeats the two firmest wordings. The deeper item is word-origin only, no new doctrine.
