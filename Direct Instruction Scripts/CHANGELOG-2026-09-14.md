# Change log — DI scripts revision, 14 September 2026

All four Word files were edited in place. Originals are preserved beside them as
`… -backup-2026-09-14.docx`. Nothing else in the folder was touched.

## What changed, in one paragraph

Every drill item whose answer is a *label* now carries a spoken cue naming the job before the item, 
without exception — including inside runs of items that are all on the same track. The cue set was 
standardised across all fifteen lessons (older short forms such as "Which act.", "Tree.", "Test it." 
and "Singular or universal." were expanded to question forms that name their own answer set). The 
One-Over-Many labels are now DEFINITION · LIST everywhere, with no surviving use of ANSWER as a label, 
and the student sentence is "That is a list, not a definition" throughout. A handful of garbled 
sentences and typos were repaired.

## How often the cue is said

Two situations, two rules.

**An announced test sequence on one track** — Lesson 1's sort, Lesson 2's definition-or-list
sequence, Lesson 3's definition tests, Lesson 4's singular/universal and splitting sequences,
Lesson 5's tree sort, Lesson 6's twenty predicables, Lesson 8's divisions, Lesson 9's judging,
Lesson 10's namings, Lesson 11's substance and first/second sorts, Lesson 12's cycles and mixed
deck — is run start to finish as a block with its labels announced immediately above it. There the
cue is said on the **first three** items, then on roughly **every fourth**, and always on the
**last**. It is also said on the first item after any break in the run (a "New rule" line, a
"Second set" transition), and on any item that is **itself a question**, where the cue is the only
thing separating the item from the teacher's own asking — so Lesson 1's *"Which act of the mind? Is
Grandmother kind?"* keeps its cue mid-sequence even though its neighbours have lost theirs.

**A list of questions** — every review block, guided practice, delayed test, spare-item bank, remedy
and mastery-test item, and the whole of the new review lesson — carries the cue on **every single
item**, including where two of the same track fall next to each other. Those items get lifted out
one at a time, and an item without its cue does not survive being lifted.

The cue tables in both conventions blocks and in the review lesson now state this rule.

## Two bugs found and fixed after the first pass

**A runaway cue expansion.** The normaliser that expanded the old short cue `Tree.` into
`Tree. Genus, species, both, or neither?` re-matched its own output, so in five paragraphs it stacked
the cue body up to twenty-six times: *"Tree. Genus, species, both, or neither? Genus, species, both,
or neither? Genus, species, both, or neither? … Food."* Three were in Unit 1 Part A (the Lesson 5
spare items, most visibly the fourth tree under "For 2") and two in Part B. All five are repaired and
every file is now checked for repeated cue bodies.

**The cue repeating inside a single spoken turn.** The first pass put the cue on every item, which is
right for a list but wrong inside one breath. The end of Lesson 5 read *"(9). Tree. Genus, species,
both, or neither? Dog. → Species. Tree. Genus, species, both, or neither? Animal. → Both. Tree. Genus,
species, both, or neither? Living thing. → Genus…"* — four beginnings of the same question in one
line. The rule is now: **the cue is said once per turn, not once per item.** Where several items run
together in one paragraph, the cue at the head covers the run; a new turn brings it back. Spare-item
banks are exempt — their entries are separated by slashes and get lifted out one at a time, so each
keeps its own cue. 55 repeats were removed across the four files.

## The review lesson

It covers Lessons 1–5 only: the three acts, the Meno, definition or list, definition testing and
counterexamples, subject and predicate, singular and universal, and the tree. **Nothing from Lesson 6

Lesson 6 as well as after, and the drafter's notes say what to do if a child volunteers one.

questions**, narrated once and not drilled; three mixed cycles that rotate the cue on almost every
item; the three collisions (list against too wide, the Fido trap and its link to singular, and the
hinge with the long subject); delayed tests in the fused form; a Deeper block for (12); a mastery note
with seven branch remedies; a cued spare-item bank; drafter's notes; and the cumulative review block
through Lesson 5.
