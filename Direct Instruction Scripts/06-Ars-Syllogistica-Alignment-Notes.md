# Ars Syllogistica — Alignment with the Sources

Findings from reading the app (index.html) against Andres, Poinsot's Outlines, and John of St. Thomas. Overall verdict: the app is squarely in the traditional camp (existential import granted, square of opposition intact, scholastic vocabulary) and is a good fit. Divergences below, with suggested changes where warranted.

## 1. Fourth figure and subaltern moods — divergence

**App:** "Traditional account (existential import): 24 valid mood-figure pairs, including the five subaltern (weakened) moods." Figures 1–4, with figure 4 moods Bramantip, Camenes, Dimaris, Fesapo, Fresison, Camenos; subalterns Barbari, Celaront, Cesaro, Camestros, Camenos. The app's orientation says all moods and figures of the scholastic account are possible, including the fourth figure and the weakened moods.

**Sources:** Andres teaches three figures; his Lesson 15 shows all syllogisms reduce to the universal syllogisms of the first figure, and Lesson 17's opening notes Aristotle's claim that every syllogism is reduced to one of the three figures. Poinsot's Outlines likewise treats modes in first, second, and third figure only (the Thomistic tradition treats fourth-figure patterns as indirect moods of the first).

**Handling in this program:** L29 teaches the three-figure doctrine with a one-panel note framing the fourth figure as scholastic bookkeeping the app uses; nothing the app calls valid is invalid on the sources' account, so no student confusion about *validity* arises — only about *counting*.

**Suggested app change:** add an "Aristotelian mode" toggle that (a) presents fourth-figure syllogisms as indirect first-figure moods (or excludes them from generation), and (b) optionally excludes the five weakened moods, which Aristotle does not count because their conclusions are needlessly weak. Even a glossary note ("Why some books count three figures") would serve.

## 2. "Proposition" vs "statement" — terminological only

**App:** 112 uses of 'proposition', copula-and-quantity standard form. **Andres:** 'statement' throughout, though Lesson 8 itself says "the statement, or proposition," which licenses the mapping. **Handling:** taught as synonyms at L20. **Suggested app change (optional):** a glossary line "proposition = statement" — nothing more needed.

## 3. Obversion and contraposition — additions beyond Andres

**App:** the Immediate Inference set drills conversion, obversion, and contraposition, and passing it gates the Third Act. **Andres:** conversion only (four rules, Lesson 12). Obversion/contraposition are later scholastic-and-modern textbook refinements; they are consistent with the sources (built on the square plus indefinite terms, which Andres does teach at Lesson 8), but not in them. **Handling:** L24 teaches both as "extra tools the app adds," so students meet nothing unsourced unawares. **Suggested app change:** none required; a note in the set's study text that Aristotle teaches conversion and the rest are the tradition's polish would be honest and cheap.

## 4. Modal Propositions gate the Third Act — pacing concern

**App:** Second Act = Diagramming Propositions (V), Immediate Inference (VI), Modal Propositions (VII); PREREQS make Third Act sets depend on all of V–VII. **Andres:** no modal treatment in the second-act lessons (8–11); modals are genuinely harder and, in the Organon's own order, modal syllogistic is advanced material. A student following the primary text would be blocked from the syllogism by material the text never teaches.

**Handling:** L26 teaches the four modes (Poinsot enrichment) so the gate can be passed as shipped.

**Suggested app change (recommended):** make Modal Propositions non-gating — exactly the treatment the app already gives Predicables and Categories in the First Act ("do not gate the path — take your time with them, and deepen them as you go"). Change PREREQS for sets 8–17 from [5,6,7] to [5,6], and move set 7 to the "deepen as you go" sentence in the orientation. This matches both the sources' order of teaching and the app's own stated philosophy for enrichment sets.

## 5. Venn diagrams — modern tool, traditionally calibrated

**App:** Venn diagrams throughout, with the ⊗ mark for traditional existential import; feedback diagrams accompany every answer; the app explicitly warns that on the modern reading the weakened moods fail, and instructs placing the × per the traditional account. **Sources:** no diagrams, but no conflict — the app has carefully bent the modern tool to the traditional doctrine. **Handling:** L25 teaches the conventions as pictures of what statements say. **Suggested change:** none. This is a strength.

## 6. Small points

- The app's letters convention (capitals = general terms, lower-case/proper names = singular) is seeded at L4 and L20; singulars treated as universals with existential import ("one thing, wholly included or wholly shut out") is consistent with the tradition's handling of singular propositions.
- The app's pass/mastery/spaced-review machinery (pass = 9/10 at Level III+ over two days; mastery at Level V; 1-3-7-16-35-day review boxes) is effectively Engelmann-compatible: mastery criterion, distributed practice, delayed testing. The program leans on it deliberately from L7 on.
- App gating (Division + Definition open Act II; Predicables/Categories non-gating) matches this program's Unit 1 emphasis exactly; the lesson sequence was aligned to it.
- Before drafting L26, open the Modal Propositions set once and note the exact question style and the modal vocabulary used, so the DI lesson drills the same discriminations the set tests. Same for Immediate Inference before L24 (confirm whether it uses the names 'simple conversion' / 'per accidens' / 'obverse' / 'contrapositive' in feedback).
