# Unit 3 Briefs — Bridge to the Third Act (Lessons 28–30)

---

## LESSON 28 — From Known to Unknown

**Objective.** Students can say what an argument is (premises + conclusion), identify premises and conclusion in short passages, and retell the slave-boy scene as an example of reasoning.

**Doctrinal material.** The third operation, that of reasoning, by which reason proceeds from what is known to the investigation of things that are unknown; we must proceed from some known truth to which the intellect assents in order to have certitude about something not yet known. The second act is ordered to the third: there is no reasoning without statements already in hand. Meno review completed: Socrates leads the slave boy by ordered questions from truths he knows to a truth he did not know — reasoning made visible; also the Meno's closing lesson: without the "what is it," we cannot settle the properties (why definitions came first).

**Example sequences.** Premise/conclusion labeling in ≥8 tiny arguments ("All fish live in water. Trout are fish. So trout live in water."); conclusion-signal words (so, therefore, because — 'because' marks a premise: minimal-difference drill). No validity judgments yet — labeling only.

**Tracks.** T7, T8, T9 all active as review.

**Deeper.** Find the unstated premise in "Socrates is a man, so Socrates is mortal."

**Mastery note.** Premises and conclusion labeled instantly; slave-boy retold in three sentences.

## LESSON 29 — First Sight of the Syllogism

**Objective.** Students can identify the three terms of a syllogism (major, minor, middle informally: "the go-between"), write one in letters, and know the app's figure conventions.

**Doctrinal material.** A syllogism is a complex expression in which, certain things being given, something else necessarily follows. Three statements, three terms, each term appearing twice; the middle term appears in both premises and vanishes from the conclusion — it is the go-between that connects the other two. Letters as schematic terms (Every M is P; Every S is M; so Every S is P) — matching the app's letters mode: capital letters stand for general terms.

**App-convention note (teach in one panel).** The premises can arrange their terms in different patterns called figures. Aristotle and our books count three figures, and show that every syllogism reduces to them; the scholastic tradition the app follows also lists a fourth figure and five weakened moods — the app will accept and drill these. When the app says "fourth figure," it is the scholastic bookkeeping of patterns our books treat as indirect forms of the first. Nothing the app calls valid is invalid by our books' account, and full figure/mood study belongs to third-act lessons to come.

**Example sequences.** (a) Find-the-middle-term ≥8. (b) English→letters ≥6. (c) "Does it follow?" intuition items ≥6 (obvious Barbara-type positives; obvious non-sequitur negatives) — intuition only, no rules yet.

**App.** Tour **The Diagram Workshop** (unscored): load the Barbara preset, watch the three-circle diagram; 10 minutes play.

**Tracks.** T8, T9.

**Deeper.** Try the Bramantip preset in the Workshop and say which premise-pattern looks "backwards" — seeds the figure discussion.

**Mastery note.** Middle term found unerringly; letters translation firm.

## LESSON 30 — Mastery Test 4 + Readiness Review

**Test content.** Cumulative over all units: three acts; definition build + counterexample; categories sort; A/E/I/O; standard form; full square TRUE/FALSE/UNKNOWN; conversion/obversion/contraposition tool-choice; modal four-way sort; premise/conclusion labeling; middle-term identification.

**Readiness checklist for Third Act app work (all should be yes):**
- Standard form automatic in both directions.
- Square inferences with UNKNOWN handled correctly.
- A-conversion weakening and O-refusal automatic.
- Diagram conventions (shading, ×, ⊗) read and drawn.
- Letters mode comfortable.
- App: First Act sets Division & Definition passed; all Second Act sets passed; Predicables & Categories at least in steady review.

**Celebration.** Open the app's Third Act together; the syllogism sets (Diagramming Syllogisms onward) become the ongoing practice spine, with future third-act DI lessons (a second series) teaching figures, moods, reduction, and then demonstration, dialectic, and fallacy per Andres Lessons 12–29.
